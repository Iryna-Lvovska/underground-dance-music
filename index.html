Welcome to the World of Underground Dance Music

Discover the sounds of UK Garage, Bassline and Speed Garage.

The Roots of UK Garage

UK Garage emerged in Britain during the 1990s, influenced by American Garage House, House music and the growing UK rave scene. It developed its own identity through shuffled rhythms, chopped vocals, soulful samples and distinctive basslines. The genre became a major part of British club culture and inspired several related styles.

Speed Garage: A Heavier Evolution

Speed Garage is a subgenre of UK Garage that became popular during the late 1990s. It is known for its heavier, often darker basslines, pitched-up vocals, chopped samples and energetic rhythms. Compared with other UK Garage styles, Speed Garage often has a tougher sound and a stronger emphasis on the bass.

Bassline: The Northern Sound

Bassline developed from UK Garage and became particularly popular in Northern England. Sheffield played an important role in its history, with Niche nightclub becoming closely associated with the scene. Bassline is recognised for its prominent, often wobbly basslines, energetic beats and distinctive club sound.

What's the Difference?

  • UK Garage: The wider genre, known for shuffled beats, soulful vocals and varied basslines.
  • Speed Garage: A heavier UK Garage subgenre, with darker bass, chopped vocals and energetic rhythms.
  • Bassline: A related style with a strong focus on prominent bass, energetic rhythms and its own Northern club identity.

The Clubs That Made History

Underground dance music was shaped not only by DJs and producers, but also by the clubs and club nights that brought people together. Different cities developed their own sounds, creating scenes that influenced British dance music for generations.

Niche – Sheffield

Niche became one of the most important clubs in the history of Northern Bassline. Its DJs embraced heavier Garage sounds and developed a distinctive style. The club became closely associated with the emerging Bassline scene, attracting people from across the country.

Twice As Nice – London

Twice As Nice was one of the influential UK Garage club nights of the late 1990s. Its Sunday sessions at the Colosseum in Vauxhall became a major part of London's Garage culture.

Frog and Nightgown – London

This venue on the Old Kent Road was an important part of London's early morning club scene. DJs played Garage and House records for crowds who wanted to keep dancing after other clubs had closed.

Casa Loco – Leeds

Casa Loco helped bring Garage and Bassline sounds to audiences in Leeds, contributing to the wider Northern club scene.

Boilerhouse – Bradford

Boilerhouse was another important venue in the Northern dance music scene, helping bring underground Garage sounds to clubbers in the region.

The Music Lives On

These sounds continue to influence DJs, producers and club culture today. Old-school classics are still played alongside modern productions, keeping the underground spirit alive. Whether through UK Garage, Speed Garage or Bassline, the distinctive British sound continues to evolve and attract new generations of listeners.
